# Engineered Remediation Technologies for Groundwater

Where natural attenuation or biological enhancement alone won't meet
site cleanup goals within an acceptable timeframe, several engineered
technologies are commonly deployed.

## Pump-and-Treat
The longest-established approach: groundwater is extracted via wells,
treated above ground (air stripping, activated carbon, advanced
oxidation), and either discharged or reinjected. Pump-and-treat is
effective at hydraulic containment (preventing plume migration) but
is often slow and expensive to achieve full mass removal, particularly
where sorbed or low-permeability-zone contaminant mass continues to
diffuse back into the flowing groundwater long after active pumping
begins — a phenomenon known as the "tailing" effect.

## Permeable Reactive Barriers (PRBs)
A PRB is a passive, in-ground treatment zone installed perpendicular
to groundwater flow, containing a reactive medium (commonly zero-valent
iron for chlorinated solvents, or organic carbon substrates to
stimulate anaerobic degradation) that treats the plume as it passes
through under the natural hydraulic gradient — no pumping required.
PRBs are attractive for long-term, low-maintenance containment but
require accurate characterization of the plume's vertical and lateral
extent to ensure it doesn't bypass the barrier.

## In-Situ Chemical Oxidation (ISCO)
ISCO injects a strong oxidant (permanganate, persulfate, hydrogen
peroxide/Fenton's reagent, or ozone) directly into the contaminated
zone to chemically destroy the contaminant in place. ISCO can achieve
rapid mass reduction in the treatment zone but is typically most
effective on the more concentrated source area rather than a dilute,
widespread dissolved plume, and can temporarily alter aquifer
geochemistry (pH, redox conditions) in ways that need to be accounted
for in follow-up monitoring.

## In-Situ Chemical Reduction (ISCR)
Related to ISCO but using reducing agents (e.g., zero-valent iron
particles injected directly into the subsurface) rather than
oxidants, ISCR is used for contaminants susceptible to reductive
transformation, such as certain chlorinated solvents and some metals.

## Soil Vapor Extraction (SVE) and Air Sparging
For volatile contaminants, SVE extracts contaminated vapor from the
unsaturated zone under vacuum, while air sparging injects air below
the water table to volatilize dissolved contaminants and/or stimulate
aerobic biodegradation; the two are frequently combined so vapors
driven out of the saturated zone by sparging are captured by the
vapor extraction system rather than migrating uncontrolled.

## Technology Selection
Choice of technology depends on contaminant type and concentration,
site geology (especially heterogeneity and the presence of
low-permeability zones storing back-diffusing mass), depth to
groundwater, receptor proximity and cleanup timeline, and cost —
often several technologies are combined in sequence (e.g., ISCO or
ISCR for source-area mass reduction, followed by MNA or PRB for the
residual dissolved plume).
